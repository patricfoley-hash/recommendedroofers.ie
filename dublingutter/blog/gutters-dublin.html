What "gutters Dublin" actually covers

The roofline is one system: the gutter itself, the fascia board it fixes to, the soffit underneath, the outlets, the downpipes, and the gully or drain the water finally reaches. When Dublin homeowners search for gutters, the fault is almost always somewhere in that chain rather than in the gutter alone.

We handle the whole roofline as Recommended Roofing & Guttering's Dublin guttering service — repairs, cleaning, full replacement, fascia and soffit, downpipes and commercial work. Why Dublin gutters fail the way they do

Dublin's housing stock is unusually varied for a city its size, and the address usually predicts the fault before we go up the ladder.

Georgian and Victorian redbrick — Rathmines, Phibsborough, Drumcondra, Portobello, Ranelagh. Original cast iron, often more than a century old and usually worth saving. Surface rust that has pitted the metal is repairable with welding and restoration; rust through the base of the section is not, and needs matching replacement sections rather than a full strip.

1930s and 1940s semis — Cabra, Crumlin, Terenure, Clontarf. uPVC fitted decades ago is now brittle from UV, with joint seals hardened past the point of resealing. A single failed joint is a repair; a run that cracks the moment you flex it is a replacement.

1970s estates — Tallaght, Clondalkin, Coolock, Ballymun. Standard 112mm half-round that is frequently undersized for the roof area it serves. These are the calls where the gutter overflows every heavy shower even when it has just been cleaned, because no amount of clearing changes the fact that the profile cannot carry the volume. Upsizing to a 125mm deep-flow profile is the fix.

Newer developments — Citywest, Clonsilla, Adamstown. Generally sound, and the calls here are blockages and the occasional bracket that has worked loose.

Coastal exposure — Howth, Sutton, Malahide, Clontarf, Sandymount, Dun Laoghaire. Wind loading these gables take is well above what inland Dublin sees. Bracket spacing matters more here, and a run fitted to inland spec will pull away on an exposed coastal elevation within a few winters.

Trees change the schedule rather than the system. Rathgar, Terenure, Clontarf, Mount Merrion and Castleknock are the streets where a second clear in spring earns its money, because mature trees within about fifteen metres of the roof drop enough through the year to block outlets twice.

How often should Dublin gutters be cleared?

Once a year for most houses, in late autumn once the leaves are down. A second clear in spring pays for itself in three situations: mature trees within about fifteen metres of the roof, a north-facing roof that holds moss through the winter, or any run that has overflowed in the previous two years.

Clearing means the gutter, the outlets and the downpipes, then flushing the whole system with water so we know it runs. Clearing a gutter and leaving a blocked downpipe achieves nothing beyond giving you the same overflow in a fortnight.

Repair or replace? The honest test

We repair before we replace, and we say plainly on site which one your gutter needs. The line sits roughly here:

  • Repair territory — one or two leaking joints, a single sagging bracket, a short damaged section, an outlet that has come loose, a downpipe that has detached from a hopper.
  • Replacement territory — a run that sags between every bracket, uPVC that cracks along its length when flexed, more patches than gutter, a profile that overflows even when perfectly clean, or a fascia so rotted that a new bracket has nothing to bite into.

That last one is the trap. Where the fascia has gone soft, fitting a new gutter to it is money wasted — the bracket screws pull out inside a year. Gutter, fascia and soffit need doing in one visit, which is more work than a gutter alone but considerably cheaper than doing the same job twice. Fascia and soffit work is priced with the guttering when they're done together for exactly this reason.

What we replace with, and why

New guttering goes in as uPVC, aluminium or cast iron, matched to what is already there and to what the house can carry. Colours match in the common profiles — black, brown, grey and white — and heritage sections are matched to existing on period properties.

Well-fitted uPVC gives thirty years or more on a Dublin house. Aluminium considerably longer. Cast iron on a period property is usually worth repairing in matching sections rather than replacing wholesale, because the original ironwork often outlasts anything modern fitted alongside it.

Every full replacement carries our Lifetime Leak Guarantee alongside the 10-year written workmanship guarantee. Repairs and partial work carry the 10-year only — we never blur the two, because promising a lifetime guarantee on a repair promises something no honest gutter contractor can stand over.

Storm damage and insurance

Storm damage to guttering is usually covered under the storm peril of a standard home insurance policy, subject to the excess. Wear-and-tear, blockages and age-related failure are not, which is where most rejected claims come from.

If a gutter has come away in a named storm, photograph it immediately from the ground before any repair work starts, and get a written report describing the damage and its cause. Insurers ask for both, and repairing first usually costs the claim.
