Why fascia and soffit are almost always done with the gutter

The gutter is screwed to the fascia. If the board behind it has gone soft, there is nothing for a new bracket to bite into, and a new gutter fitted to rotten timber will pull away inside a winter. On the Dublin housing stock the firm works on most often — 1930s and 1940s semis in Cabra, Crumlin, Terenure and Clontarf — the original timber fascia has frequently taken forty years of eastern-coast weather and failed at the back edge where it meets the felt.

Where that is the case, the honest quote is one visit that strips the gutter, renews the fascia and soffit in uPVC, and refits new gutter, brackets, unions and outlets to sound board. More work on the day, but the only version of the job that stands over itself for ten years.

How do I tell if my fascia has rotted?

Four signs that mean the board has gone and a repaint will not save it:

  • The gutter is sagging between brackets, especially where the bracket screws can be seen pulling out of the fascia.
  • Paint is lifting or flaking in long strips rather than small patches, with soft or spongy timber underneath when pressed.
  • Dark staining or green growth down the fascia below the gutter line, pointing to water tracking behind the gutter onto the board.
  • Visible gaps between the fascia and the soffit, or a soffit that has started to drop at one end.

On a coastal gable in Howth, Sutton, Malahide, Clontarf, Sandymount or Dun Laoghaire the back edge of the fascia takes the worst of it, and the rot often starts there before anything shows from the ground. That is why the survey is done from a ladder, not a driveway.

What is the difference between fascia and soffit?

The fascia is the vertical board that runs along the ends of the rafters at the edge of the roof. It carries the gutter and closes the roof off at the eaves. The soffit is the horizontal board tucked underneath the fascia, bridging the gap from the fascia back to the wall, and it usually carries the vents that let the roof space breathe.

Both are replaced in uPVC on modern renewals, colour-matched to black, brown, grey or white in the common profiles. Ventilated soffit is standard on anything but a cold-roof extension, because blocking the eaves vents is the quickest way to put condensation into a loft.
