How much does gutter cleaning cost in Dublin 15?

Cleaning is quoted per job after a look at the property because the honest drivers — how tall the eaves are, whether a ladder will reach or a tower is needed, and how much material is sitting up there — are not visible from a form. As an indicative Dublin market range, a straightforward semi-detached clean sits in the low-hundreds; a large detached with awkward access, a conservatory to work around, or a three-storey rear elevation moves higher. We confirm the exact figure free after the survey.

What we will not do is quote a flat "€X per house" from the kerb. A three-bed semi on Coolmine Woods and a four-bed detached on Beechpark Avenue are not the same job, and pretending they are is how customers end up with add-ons on the day. The written quote and no call-out fee approach applies to every visit, cleaning included.

What's actually included in a clean?

A proper clean is three tasks done in sequence, not one:

  1. The gutter run itself — leaves, moss, silt and grit lifted out by hand and bagged, not blown into the garden or flushed down the pipes to block them a metre lower.
  2. Every outlet — the drop from gutter to downpipe cleared, because this is where blockages actually form on a Dublin house.
  3. The downpipes — rodded or flushed through to the gully, with hoppers checked on the older Castleknock and Carpenterstown houses that still have them.

We finish by running water down the full system so we can see it clears at the gully. If a joint drips, a bracket has pulled, or the fall has been lost on part of the run, we tell you on the day with a photograph — not as a scare tactic, but because you paid for someone to go up and look, and the point of going up is to come back down with information.

When should Dublin 15 gutters be cleaned?

Once a year for most houses, in late autumn once the leaves are down. The Dublin 15 exceptions worth knowing:

  • Mature-tree roads — the older parts of Castleknock village, Laurel Lodge, Roselawn and the tree-lined stretches of Carpenterstown need two clears a year. Beech, sycamore and lime are the usual culprits, and one autumn clean leaves you exposed to spring blossom drop and helicopter seeds.
  • North-facing roofs — anywhere the rear pitch faces north and holds moss through the winter benefits from a spring clean as well, because moss dislodged by frost ends up in the gutter.
  • Anything that overflowed last winter — if a run overflowed once, it will overflow again unless the cause has been dealt with. Clean it, then diagnose it.
  • Newer estates on open ground — Ongar, Hansfield, Tyrrelstown, parts of Clonsilla — usually one clear is genuinely enough. We will say so rather than upsell you a schedule you do not need.

Book the autumn clean for late October through late November. Any earlier and half the leaves are still on the trees; any later and the first heavy rain has already tested the system for you.

Why does my gutter overflow even after cleaning?

Three causes, and cleaning only fixes one. A blockage or lost fall dams the run at a single point — that is what cleaning solves. A failed joint or a pulled bracket lets water drip behind the gutter onto the fascia, which looks like an overflow but is a repair. And on many of the 1970s Blanchardstown estates the original 112mm half-round profile is simply undersized for the roof area it serves, so it spills over in heavy rain no matter how clean it is. That last one is not fixable by cleaning — the run has to be upsized to a 125mm deep-flow profile.

If your gutter is overflowing after a recent clean, get someone up there to tell you which of the three is happening. Our gutter repair service across Dublin covers the first two; the third is a full gutter replacement job, and we will say so plainly rather than sell you three visits that will not solve it.

What Dublin 15 housing stock means for the clean

  • Castleknock village and older Carpenterstown — mature gardens, tall trees, often three storeys at the rear where a ground-floor extension has been added. Access decides the price here more than anything else.
  • 1970s and 1980s estates in Corduff, Coolmine and Mulhuddart — standard-profile uPVC, often original, with brittle joints. A clean frequently exposes a joint that has been leaking quietly for a year. Cheaper to reseal it during the same visit than to book a separate repair.
  • Clonsilla, Ongar and Tyrrelstown — 1990s to 2010s stock, generally sound, deeper profiles, better falls. The calls here are almost always straightforward blockages and the occasional bracket.
  • Newer Hansfield and Barnwell — young landscaping, less debris, but the flat-fronted terraces catch driven rain and the downpipes take a hammering. The downpipe check matters more than the gutter clean.
