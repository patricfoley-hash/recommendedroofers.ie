When should you replace rather than repair?

Dublin Gutter repairs before we replace. We say so plainly, and we say when a repair will not hold. The decision usually comes down to how much of the run has failed.

A repair is the right answer when:

  • One or two joints are leaking on an otherwise sound run
  • A single bracket has pulled or a short section has cracked
  • The fall has been lost at a joint and can be reset
  • A downpipe or outlet is blocked, split or detached

Replacement is the honest answer when:

  • The run sags between almost every bracket, which means the fascia or the gutter itself has gone
  • uPVC has become brittle through UV and cracks when you touch it (common on 1930s and 1940s Dublin semis in Cabra, Crumlin, Terenure and Clontarf where the original uPVC fitted decades ago is now past resealing)
  • The profile is simply too small for the roof area, which is the usual story on 1970s estates in Tallaght, Clondalkin, Coolock and Ballymun where 112mm half-round overflows along the whole run even when perfectly clean
  • Cast iron has rusted through the base of the section rather than just pitting the surface

That last point matters on the Georgian and Victorian redbricks of Rathmines, Phibsborough, Drumcondra, Portobello and Ranelagh. Original cast iron is often worth saving section by section rather than replacing the lot.

What's included in a Dublin gutter replacement?

A full replacement from Dublin Gutter covers the whole system, not just the trough that catches the water:

  • Removing and disposing of the old guttering, brackets and downpipes
  • New guttering in your chosen material and profile, colour-matched (black, brown, grey or white in the common profiles)
  • New brackets at the correct spacing for the run (tighter on exposed coastal gables in Howth, Sutton, Malahide, Sandymount and Dun Laoghaire, where wind loading pulls a run that would hold inland)
  • New unions, stop ends and outlets
  • New downpipes, hoppers and shoes
  • Checking the pipe actually discharges to a gully rather than onto the ground against a wall
  • A water flush of the full system before we leave, so you know it clears rather than being told it does

Where the fascia board behind the gutter has gone soft, there is nothing for a new bracket to bite into. In that case gutter, fascia and soffit get done in one visit. It is more work on the day, but considerably cheaper than coming back and taking the new gutter off again to replace the board underneath.

Which material should you choose?

Three materials cover almost every Dublin house.

uPVC is the standard choice for the modern housing stock, from 1930s semis to newer developments in Citywest, Clonsilla and Adamstown. Well-fitted uPVC should give thirty years or more. It is the cheapest option and the quickest to install.

Aluminium costs about 30% more than uPVC and lasts considerably longer. It holds its shape under load and is a sensible choice on exposed coastal elevations and on larger properties where the extra rigidity earns its keep.

Cast iron belongs on period houses where the look matters and the planning context demands it. Georgian and Victorian redbricks in Rathmines, Drumcondra and Portobello often carry original cast iron that is worth repairing in matching sections rather than stripping. Where full replacement is needed, cast iron prices at €75 to €90 per metre installed.

When an older Dublin gutter overflows along the whole run

This is the single most misdiagnosed Dublin guttering problem. On many 1970s estates, the fitted profile is 112mm half-round that is simply undersized for the roof area it serves. No amount of cleaning or joint-sealing fixes it, because nothing has failed. The gutter is doing what it can with the capacity it has.

The fix is to upsize to a 125mm deep-flow profile, which carries roughly twice the volume of water. A repair quote on an undersized gutter is money burned; the honest answer is replacement at the larger profile.

How to choose a Dublin gutter contractor

Guttering has the same trust problem as the rest of the trade. Subcontracted crews, verbal quotes, and no comeback when it leaks again the following winter. Three checks separate a genuine contractor from the rest:

  1. Directly employed crews, not subcontractors. Ask the question plainly. Dublin Gutter does not subcontract; the people on your roofline are our people.
  2. The guarantee in writing before you sign. Not promised afterwards, not verbal. Dublin Gutter hands over a 10-year written workmanship guarantee, plus the Lifetime Leak Guarantee on every full replacement, before contracts are signed.
  3. Insurance, VAT and registration on request. €6.5M public liability, €13M employer liability, VAT number IE04393055LH, and CIRI registration. We encourage people to check them before we are appointed.

Membership of the National Guild of Master Craftsmen is a supporting credential rather than the main one; the guarantee, the directly employed crews and the 5.0★ across 20+ Google reviews do the real work.
