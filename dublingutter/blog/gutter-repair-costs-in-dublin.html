Gutter Repair Costs in Dublin

Costs 11 min read 2026-09-02

Gutter repair costs in Dublin typically run from €95–€180 for a clear and reseal, €180–€350 for a bracket and section repair, €750–€1,400 for a full run replacement, and €2,500–€4,500 for a whole-house replacement in uPVC. The right figure depends on whether the fault is a blockage, a failed joint, or an undersized profile. Prices are indicative — a free survey confirms the actual number.

The short version

  • A clear-and-reseal on a single joint sits at the bottom of the repair scale; a full elevation repair is priced as replacement work because that is effectively what it is.
  • uPVC installed on 1930s Dublin semis runs €50–€65 per metre supplied and fitted, aluminium €65–€75, cast iron €75–€90 — the profile chosen matters more than the length.
  • Water spilling over the front edge on a 1970s Tallaght or Clondalkin estate is usually an undersized 112mm half-round, and no repair fixes it — upsizing to 125mm deep-flow does.
  • A standard three-bed semi carries roughly 15–20 metres of guttering front and rear combined, so multiply the per-metre rate to sanity-check any whole-house quote.
  • Cast iron on Rathmines or Phibsborough redbrick is usually worth repairing in matching sections rather than ripped off wholesale — surface rust is repairable, rust through the base of the section is not.
  • Every full replacement carries a Lifetime Leak Guarantee alongside the 10-year written workmanship guarantee; repairs carry the 10-year cover only.

Why is water spilling over the front edge?

Three faults produce the same symptom, and telling them apart is the whole job before any money is spent.

A blockage or a lost fall dams the run at one point. Leaves, moss shed off the slates, and silt build gradually against an outlet or at a low spot where a bracket has dropped. Water backs up behind the dam and takes the shortest route out — over the front lip. You will usually see it in one place on the run, not along the whole length. This is a cleaning job, sometimes with a bracket reset behind it.

A failed joint or bracket drips behind the gutter, not over the front. If the fascia below is stained or the wall behind the downpipe is wet, the water is escaping at the back of the profile rather than the front. Joint seals harden with UV exposure — Dublin's older uPVC, fitted in the 1980s and 1990s across Cabra, Crumlin and Terenure, is often past the point where a reseal will hold, and the union needs replacing.

An undersized profile overflows along the whole run even when the gutter is spotless. Many 1970s estates — Tallaght, Clondalkin, Coolock, Ballymun — were fitted with 112mm half-round that never had the capacity for the roof pitched above it. In a heavy Dublin shower, the water sheet coming off the slates simply exceeds what the profile can carry, and it sheets over the front for the length of the elevation. No repair solves it; the fix is a larger profile.

The reason for going up and looking rather than pricing from the ground is that these three faults produce identical spillover but have prices an order of magnitude apart.

When does a repair stop making sense?

A cracked joint, a sagging bracket, a short damaged section — these are repairs. Money spent on them buys another twenty years of the gutter you have.

A run that sags between every bracket, cracks along its length, or has more patches than gutter has stopped being economical to repair. On brittle 1980s uPVC — common across Cabra, Crumlin and the older parts of Terenure and Clontarf — the material itself has lost the flex to hold a new bracket without splitting. Refixing to a full elevation on that gutter costs most of what a new elevation costs, and it carries repair-scope cover rather than the Lifetime Leak Guarantee that comes with a full replacement.

The honest rule: if the repair quote is more than 60% of the replacement quote for the same elevation, replace it. You get a longer-lived gutter, tighter joints, and the leak guarantee that only comes with new work.

What about the fascia behind the gutter?

Where the board a gutter is screwed to has gone soft — a common finding on 1930s and 1940s semis where the original timber fascia was never capped — there is nothing for a new bracket to bite into. Fitting new gutter to failed board is a wasted visit.

In that case gutter, fascia and soffit get done together in one visit. The gutter has to come off to reach the board anyway, so doing both at once is considerably cheaper than doing them in two separate visits six months apart. Expect roughly €85–€120 per metre for the combined roofline replacement on uPVC, all in, and confirm on survey — the driver is whether the rafter feet underneath are sound or need attention as well.

uPVC is the standard fit on most Dublin semis and terraced houses and, well fitted, gives thirty years or more. Aluminium is worth the premium on exposed coastal gables — Howth, Sutton, Malahide, Sandymount, Dun Laoghaire — where wind loading pulls uPVC away from the fascia faster than inland runs.

Surface rust that has pitted the outside is repairable; rust through the base of the section is not. We repair in matching profile rather than ripping off period character wholesale.

How do I tell if my Dublin gutter needs replacement rather than repair?

Six signs the run is past economical repair, drawn from Dublin housing stock we see every week:

  1. Sags visible between every bracket — the profile has lost its structural memory, common on 40-year-old uPVC.
  2. Overflow along the entire front run in heavy rain, gutter clean — an undersized profile, typical on 1970s 112mm half-round.
  3. Cracks running along the length of a section rather than at joints — UV embrittlement, no seal will hold.
  4. More than three separate patches on one elevation — the run is telling you it is finished.
  5. Fascia stained or rotted behind the gutter along its length — water has been tracking behind for years.
  6. Brackets pulling through the fascia in more than two places — the substrate has failed, not just the fittings.

One of these on its own is usually a repair. Three or more together is replacement territory.

Storm damage and insurance

Storm damage to guttering is usually covered under the storm peril of a standard Irish home insurance policy, subject to the excess. Wear and tear, blockage and age-related failure are not. If a gutter has come away in a named storm — Éowyn, Ashley or any subsequent — photograph it immediately from the ground and from an upstairs window, and get a written report before any repair starts. We produce that report as part of the survey at no extra charge.
