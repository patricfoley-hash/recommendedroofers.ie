Gutter Repair Costs in Dublin

Costs 10 min read 2026-09-02

Gutter repair in Dublin starts at around €500 for a couple of leaking joints on one run, with most jobs landing between €1,000 and €1,900 for a sagging run realigned, brackets renewed and joints resealed. A single-run clear and reseal is cheaper; a full-house replacement on a semi runs €1,000–€1,400 in uPVC.

The short version

  • Clean and flush only: priced per job after a look at the house, with downpipes included — the honest answer on a Cabra semi is different from a three-storey Ranelagh redbrick.
  • Small repair (one run, a couple of leaking joints): €500–€800, done in a single visit.
  • Typical repair (sagging run realigned, brackets renewed, joints resealed, a downpipe sorted): €1,000–€1,900.
  • Extensive or cast iron repair on a period property: €1,900–€3,000, because welding and matching sections take time.
  • Full replacement on a standard ~20-metre semi: €1,000–€1,400 in uPVC, €1,300–€1,800 in aluminium, €2,100–€2,800 in cast iron — includes strip-out, new brackets, unions and downpipes.
  • 1970s estates in Tallaght, Clondalkin and Coolock commonly need upsizing to a 125mm deep-flow profile because the original 112mm half-round is undersized for the roof it serves.

Why is water spilling over the front of my gutter?

Water tipping over the front edge in heavy rain has three causes, and they need telling apart before any money is spent. The wrong diagnosis is the reason people pay twice.

A blockage or a lost fall dams the run at one point. Leaves, moss and roof grit collect at the low end near the outlet, or a bracket has dropped and created a new low point mid-run. Water backs up behind the dam and spills over the front lip. This is a cleaning and re-fall job, not a replacement.

A failed joint or bracket lets water drip behind the gutter onto the fascia rather than out the front. If the front of the house shows staining down the render below the gutter line, and the fascia board above is soft or blackened, this is the culprit. Sealing the union, replacing a cracked bracket, or renewing a short section of gutter fixes it.

An undersized profile is the Dublin classic that gets missed. On many 1930s semis and 1970s estates the original 112mm half-round PVC is simply too small for the roof area draining into it. Overflow happens every heavy downpour, even the day after it has been cleaned, because there is nowhere for the water to go. The fix is upsizing to a 125mm deep-flow profile, and no amount of cleaning or resealing will change the outcome.

The reason a survey matters here is that the difference between the three is not visible from a driveway. A gutter can look fine from below and be running backwards from a dropped bracket ten metres in; a clean run can be undersized by design.

How much does a gutter clean cost in Dublin?

Cleaning is priced per job after a look at the property rather than per metre, because size of house, roof height, ladder access and how much material is up there all move it more than the length of gutter does. Downpipes are included as standard — clearing a gutter and leaving a blocked downpipe achieves nothing, and it is one of the more common ways a first-time customer has been shortchanged elsewhere.

Most Dublin houses need one clear a year, in late autumn once the leaves are down. A second clear in spring earns its money on the leafier roads — Rathgar, Terenure, Clontarf, Mount Merrion, Castleknock — and on any north-facing roof that holds moss. Estates on open ground in Citywest, Clonsilla or Adamstown usually manage on the annual.

There is no call-out fee, and the quote is written before anything starts.

What does a bracket and section repair cost?

This is the middle band and where most Dublin repair jobs actually land. Typical repair work runs €1,000–€1,900 and covers realigning a sagging run, renewing the brackets that have pulled through or dropped, replacing joint seals and unions that have hardened past resealing, and sorting a downpipe if one has come loose or split.

What moves the number inside that band:

  • How much of the system is being worked on. A single elevation is at the bottom; front and rear together push toward the top.
  • The material. uPVC works are quicker than aluminium; both are considerably faster than cast iron.
  • Height and access. A two-storey semi with driveway access is straightforward. A three-storey Georgian on Portobello with cars parked tight to the kerb needs a tower rather than a ladder, and the day costs more.
  • Whether the fascia is sound. If the board a bracket is screwed to has rotted, there is nothing for a new bracket to bite into. Fascia and soffit then have to come off in the same visit, which is more work but considerably cheaper than doing it twice.

Small jobs — a couple of leaking joints on one run, no realignment, no brackets — sit at €500–€800. This is the honest answer for a single failed union on a modern semi in Adamstown, and quoting it as a €1,500 job would be dishonest.

Cast iron on a period redbrick is a different animal. Rust that has pitted the surface is repairable, often by welding and repainting rather than replacement. Rust through the base of the section is not. Extensive or cast iron repair work runs €1,900–€3,000, and the extra reflects welding time, matching sections and the care period ironwork asks for.

Every repair carries the 10-year written workmanship guarantee. The Lifetime Leak Guarantee applies to full replacements only — it is worth being clear about which one covers your job before signing.

When is it cheaper to replace the whole run?

A run that sags between every bracket, cracks along its length, or has been patched more times than it has been left alone has stopped being economical to repair. The rough test is that once a repair quote crosses about 60% of the replacement figure, you are paying twice for something that will need doing properly inside five years.

Full replacement on a standard three-bed Dublin semi carries roughly 15–20 metres of gutter across front and rear combined. Indicative installed prices, including strip-out and disposal, new brackets, unions, outlets and downpipes:

Dublin's housing stock and what usually fails

The address is a fair predictor of the fault:

  • Georgian and Victorian redbrick in Rathmines, Phibsborough, Drumcondra, Portobello and Ranelagh runs on original cast iron, often worth saving. Repair beats replacement here almost every time, and the pricing sits in the extensive band because welding and matching sections are involved.
  • 1930s and 1940s semis in Cabra, Crumlin, Terenure and Clontarf were fitted with uPVC decades ago that is now brittle from UV, with joint seals hardened past the point of resealing. A couple of unions will fail, then another two the following winter, and at some point full replacement of an elevation is cheaper than the rolling repairs.
  • 1970s estates in Tallaght, Clondalkin, Coolock and Ballymun carry standard 112mm half-round profile that is frequently undersized for the roof area. Cleaning does nothing here; upsizing to 125mm deep-flow is the actual fix.
  • Newer developments in Citywest, Clonsilla and Adamstown are generally sound — most calls are blockages and the occasional bracket.

Coastal exposure changes the maths on the northside and along the bay. Howth, Sutton, Malahide, Sandymount and Dun Laoghaire take wind loading that inland areas do not, and bracket spacing needs closing up. A run that has held fine in Tallaght for twenty years will pull away from an exposed gable in Sutton in ten.

Is the damage a claim on my home insurance?

Storm damage to guttering is usually covered under the storm peril, subject to your excess. Wear and tear, blockage and age-related failure are not, and this is where most claims get declined. If a gutter has come away in a named storm, photograph it immediately from the ground, keep any pieces, and get a written report before any repair starts. A repair invoice dated three weeks after Storm Éowyn with no supporting photos is a hard sell to a loss adjuster.
