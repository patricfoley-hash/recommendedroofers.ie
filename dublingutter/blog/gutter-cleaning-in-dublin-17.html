What gutter cleaning in Dublin 17 actually covers

A clean is not a leaf-scoop. On a Dublin 17 house it means going up to the roofline, clearing the full run of the gutter by hand, lifting silt and moss out of the outlets, rodding or flushing every downpipe to the gully, then running water through the whole system to prove it clears. If the pipe discharges onto the ground rather than into a gully — common on the older Priorswood terraces where the shoe has broken away — we flag it on the day.

We go up and look rather than pricing from the ground. The difference between a sagging bracket and a blocked outlet is not visible from a driveway, and quoting from below is how homeowners end up paying for a clean when they needed a repair, or vice versa.

For anything beyond a clear — leaking joints, pulled brackets, water tracking behind the fascia — see our gutter repairs across Dublin page. If the run is failing along its length, the honest answer is full gutter replacement, not another clean next year.

How much does gutter cleaning cost in Dublin 17?

Cleaning is priced per job after a look at the house. What moves it: the size and height of the house, whether a ladder reaches or a tower is needed, and how much material is in the gutter. A three-bed semi in Coolock with a straightforward two-storey run is at the lower end; a three-storey townhouse in Clarehall with restricted rear access sits higher.

Indicative Irish-market ranges for a residential gutter clean in 2026 run roughly €80–€180 for a standard semi and €180–€300 for larger or harder-to-access properties. Treat those as sector guidance rather than a Dublin Gutter quote — we confirm the exact figure free after a survey, in writing, before anything starts. There is no call-out fee.

Downpipes are included. So is a water test at the end. If we spot a repair while we are up there — a loose bracket, a failed union — you get a written price for it on the same visit rather than a second call-out later.

When should Dublin 17 homeowners book a clean?

Late autumn, once the leaves are actually down. Booking in September is too early; the sycamores and limes on the older Coolock roads have not finished shedding, and the gutter fills again within a fortnight. Mid-November to mid-December is the honest window for a single annual clear.

Book a second clear in spring if any of the following apply to your house:

  • Mature trees within about fifteen metres of the roof. Parts of Coolock village, the older streets around Kilmore Road and the tree-lined stretches of Malahide Road all qualify.
  • A north-facing roof that holds moss. Moss shedding into the gutter after winter blocks outlets faster than leaves do.
  • Any run that overflowed in the last two years. Overflow is a symptom, not a fixture — once a gutter has done it, it will do it again unless the underlying cause has been dealt with.

Newer developments off the Malahide Road and around Clarehall are generally on open ground with limited overhang, so one clear a year is usually enough.

Why does my Dublin 17 gutter overflow even after cleaning?

Three causes, and they need telling apart before more money is spent:

  1. A blockage or a lost fall dams the run at one point — the fix is the clean itself, plus resetting the fall if brackets have dropped.
  2. A failed joint or bracket lets water drip behind the gutter onto the fascia. From the ground it looks like an overflow; up close, the front lip is dry. That is a repair, not a cleaning fault.
  3. The profile is undersized for the roof area. The 1970s Corporation-era estates through Coolock, Priorswood and Darndale were built with 112mm half-round guttering that struggles with modern rainfall intensities. No clean fixes this. The fix is upsizing to a 125mm deep-flow profile, which is a replacement job on that elevation.

If your gutter overflowed in one of the heavier autumn downpours last year and again this year, in the same spot, on a clean run — it is almost certainly the third one. Our Dublin guttering hub walks through when repair beats replacement and when it does not.

The housing stock in Dublin 17 and what it means for your gutters

Dublin 17 covers Coolock, Priorswood, Darndale, Belcamp, Kilmore and the newer estates off the Malahide Road out towards Clarehall. Three broad categories, three different failure patterns:

  • 1970s Corporation estates — Priorswood, Darndale, parts of Coolock. Standard 112mm uPVC half-round, now fifty years old, brittle from UV and with joint seals hardened well past the point of resealing. Cleaning keeps them going; a run that has cracked along its length is finished.
  • Older Coolock village stock — pre-war and inter-war semis around the Main Street and Tonlegee Road. Often on their second or third gutter, sometimes cast iron on the front elevation still. Worth inspecting rather than assuming.
  • Newer developments — Clarehall, the estates off Malahide Road, Belcamp Lane. Generally sound; the calls here are blockages and the occasional pulled bracket after a storm.

Wind exposure is moderate rather than coastal in Dublin 17, but the estates on open ground north of the Malahide Road do catch weather off the airport approach. Bracket spacing matters on those exposed rear gables.

What happens after we clean

Every visit ends with a water test. We put a hose in the highest point of the run and watch it discharge at the gully. If it clears cleanly, we are done. If it does not, we tell you why on the spot — a partial blockage further down, a failed outlet, a downpipe that discharges into blocked ground rather than a working gully — and quote the fix in writing before we leave.

Any remedial work carried out on the day is covered by our 10-year written workmanship guarantee. A full gutter replacement, where that turns out to be the honest answer, additionally carries our Lifetime Leak Guarantee — the paperwork is handed over before contracts are signed, not promised afterwards.
